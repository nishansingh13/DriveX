The DriveX demo, step by step

A script for presenting the fault-tolerance demo. It follows the Fault-tolerance demo panel in the app exactly: eight steps, one click each, so you set the pace. Takes about two minutes.

A second way: do it by hand on disk

Shows the examiners the failures are real, not just buttons.

Delete a chunk file

  1. Open server/storage/node1 in Finder or VS Code. Delete one .chunk file.
  2. Click Download on that file in the app. The log shows "no copy found on node1" and the copy is restored.

Rename a node's folder

  1. Rename server/storage/node2 to node2_x. Within a few seconds the node2 light in the top bar goes grey, even though the database still says it is online.
  2. Download a file: it works from the other copies. Rename the folder back to restore it.

A node counts as available only when it is marked online and its folder exists. That is how both a simulated and a real failure are detected.

Questions to expect

Are these real servers?No. They are four folders on one machine, which models how distributed storage behaves. It is not protection against losing the whole computer.
Why did step 4 break the download?With two copies, one failure at a time is guaranteed. Two neighbouring nodes share a quarter of the chunks, so those chunks lose both copies. Two opposite nodes (1 and 3, or 2 and 4) would have lost nothing.
How does it know a copy is damaged?Each chunk's SHA-256 hash is stored in MongoDB when the file is uploaded. On every read the chunk is hashed again and compared.
What if both copies of a chunk are damaged?That chunk can't be recovered, because repair needs one good copy. The download is stopped and the failure is recorded.
Why two copies and not three?Each extra copy costs more disk space. Two is the smallest number that survives a failure.
